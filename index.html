About the Trust

For the public at large, without preference

The Trust was created by a deed executed on 17 August 2026 to establish a public charitable trust for the benefit of the public at large.

Its funds are applied without preference to any country, caste, creed, rural or urban area, religion or community. Its objects do not include any activity for profit within the meaning of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

Founder
Birendra Kumar Singh

Settlor, Chairman and Managing Trustee. The founder serves as Managing Trustee and President and presides over meetings of the Board of Trustees.

Our objects

What the Trust is set up to do

Taken from the objects in the trust deed. The Trustees may apply the Trust's funds to any one or more of them.

Education and learning

Schools and colleges, educational books and literature, scholarships, stipends and certificates, and conferences, lectures and discourses.

Health and medical help

Medical help and health check-ups, health camps and blood donation camps, medicines for the needy, and support for dispensaries and hospitals.

Sports

Sports academies and camps to find new talent, coaching, training and infrastructure, and recognition through prizes, rewards and scholarships.

Help for people in need

Food, shelter, education and medical care for poor people, and relief for those hit by earthquakes, floods, droughts, famines, fires and riots.

Persons with disabilities and orphans

Education, sports, learning, art and cultural facilities, and relief for the physically handicapped, blind, deaf, orphans and the disabled.

Women, children and human rights

Forums through lectures, seminars, workshops and symposia, and support for institutions working in these fields.

Culture, science and awareness

Art, culture, science and tourism, plus educational websites and multimedia programmes in India and abroad to spread awareness.

Environment and animals

Plantation and conservation of natural resources, and support for cow sheds and feed for cattle, birds and animals.

Care homes and centres

Orphanages, old age homes and similar centres for charitable purposes, and support for other institutions with similar objects.

Governance

How the Trust is run

The deed sets out how the Trust is managed and accounted for.

Board of Trustees
A minimum of two and a maximum of eleven Trustees manage the Trust.
Meetings
The Board meets at least once every calendar quarter and keeps a minute book of its proceedings.
Honorary service
Trustees hold honorary office and receive no salary or allowance, only reimbursement of actual expenses.
Accounts and audit
The financial year runs from 1 April to 31 March. A chartered accountant audits the accounts every year.
Foreign contributions
Contributions from abroad are accepted only after the registration required by foreign contribution law is obtained.
If the Trust is dissolved
Liabilities are paid first. Remaining assets go to the Central or State Government or to another registered charitable trust with similar objects.
